The Giant's Causeway:

Visions through camera, cartography, community, and computer

An experimental pilot project

This is a small digital humanities experiment, looking at artefacts from the Godfrey Bingley Collection, integrating manual mapping of artefacts with explorations of the possibilities and limitations of AI tools in cultural collection workflows, specifically with relation to georeferencing of photographs.

In many ways this experiment acts as a counterpoint to the Coin Project, which uses niche and specialist machine-learning methods tailored to their application: this uses generalist tools currently available at the University of Leeds to supplement curation work. The aim was to explore the possibilities and limitations of these tools, and potentially act as somewhat of a cautionary tale.


This project highlights the possibilities available to curators with digital tools and frameworks such as IIIF Manifests, showcases simple approaches to visualising georeferenced artefacts, and highlights where generalist tools still fall short.

Data Layers

  • Sattelite view toggle: check this box to swap from the Open Street Maps basemap to sattelite imagery of the coast.
  • Hand-curated data: this dataset shows locations along the coast, with related images from the Bingley Collection.
  • Geological Map: this layer shows the 1887 geological map of the Giant's Causeway, as part of the Geological Survey of Ireland.
  • Geological Map Legend: a scrollable snipping of the original legend from the above geological map, showing the stratigraphy. OCR was completed by CoPilot, the result is also available in the pop-up.
  • Machine generated locations: a layer of location data generated by CoPilot; the prompt was repeated three times (detail can be found in the accompanying report); you can view the triangle of variance for each artefact by clicking on the marker. This will open a polygon; each corner of the polygon shows a location suggested by CoPilot. Each datapoint also contains a pop-up with CoPilot generated archival metadata and curated links to related material.

How to Use

Please remember that this is an exploration of the possibilities and limitations of AI use in Cultural Collections, and is not intended as a finalised exhibit or resource.

  • Use the toggles in the top-right corner to show or hide different data layers.
  • Click on markers or polygons to view detailed information and related images.
  • Scroll the map to explore the area, and use the zoom controls to adjust your view.
